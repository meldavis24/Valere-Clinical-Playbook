Comprehensive Hormone-Focused Intake
1.1 · New Patient Protocol · All Service Lines
Purpose
To establish a standardized, hormone-focused intake process that ensures safe patient selection, appropriate risk stratification, and individualized treatment planning for hormone optimization, peptide therapy, longevity, and adjunct metabolic support services at Valère.
Patient Eligibility
  • Adults 18 years or older seeking optimization-focused care
  • Hormone-related symptoms, quality-of-life concerns, or preventive health goals
  • Medically stable for outpatient hormone and adjunct therapies
  • Agreement to longitudinal monitoring and follow-up requirements
Exclusion / Referral Criteria
  • Active or uncontrolled malignancy (breast, prostate, endometrial)
  • Unstable cardiovascular disease (recent MI, stroke, uncontrolled arrhythmias)
  • Known hypercoagulable disorders without specialist clearance
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Untreated severe psychiatric illness
  • Complex endocrine disorders requiring specialty management
⚑ Provider Note — Intake Flags
During intake, also document: (1) current hormone-blocking therapies (aromatase inhibitors, anti-androgens); (2) hormone-like supplements (phytoestrogens, DHEA, pregnenolone, adaptogens); (3) post-chemotherapy or radiation history — requires baseline evaluation before initiating any hormonal or peptide therapy.
Intake Components
Medical History
Past surgical history, family history (cardiovascular & hormone-sensitive cancers), medications, supplements, prior hormone/peptide use, allergies
Symptom Assessment
Fatigue, brain fog, mood, sleep, weight changes, libido, muscle mass, recovery, cognitive/stress symptoms. Validated questionnaires at provider discretion.
Reproductive & Sexual Health
Menstrual history, menopausal status, fertility goals, erectile/sexual function, contraception use, prior hormone exposure
Cardiovascular & Clot Risk
HTN, dyslipidemia, DM, smoking, BMI/waist, clotting disorders, stroke/MI/DVT/PE history
Cancer Risk Screening
Personal & family history of hormone-sensitive cancers, prior abnormal PSA/mammogram/Pap/endometrial biopsy, current screening compliance
Baseline Labs
CMP, lipid panel, CBC, thyroid, sex hormone panel (T, E2, Prog, SHBG), PSA (men), HbA1c/IR markers, additional at provider discretion
Risk Stratification
Low Risk
Eligible for standard hormone optimization protocols
Moderate Risk
May proceed with enhanced monitoring and documented rationale
High Risk
Requires specialist clearance or referral prior to initiation

Estradiol Therapy
2.1 · Oral / Transdermal / Vaginal / Pellet · Adult Female Patients
Clinical Oversight Principles
  • Estradiol decline may be gradual or abrupt; often follows earlier progesterone decline
  • Decision-making guided by symptom patterns and physiologic context — not lab values alone
  • Route of administration materially impacts systemic exposure, risk profile, and response
  • Uterine status and endometrial safety must be considered in all cases of systemic exposure
  • Therapy aims at physiologic signaling restoration, not supraphysiologic replacement
Route-Level Oversight
RoutePrimary RoleKey Considerations
Oral EstradiolSystemic symptom managementHepatic first-pass metabolism; impacts SHBG, triglycerides, clotting factors; cardiometabolic/thrombotic risk assessment required
TransdermalSystemic symptom managementBypasses hepatic first-pass; preferred when cardiometabolic or clot-risk factors are present
VaginalLocal urogenital supportPrimarily for GSM/vulvovaginal atrophy; typically does not require progesterone co-therapy at low doses
PelletSustained systemic deliveryLong duration of action; limited dose adjustability; requires careful patient selection and longitudinal monitoring
⚑ Risk Stratification — 3 Key Situations
(1) Family or personal history of malignancy (hormone-sensitive cancers) — document and heighten monitoring; (2) Current use of non-hormonal hormone-like supplements (phytoestrogens, DHEA, adaptogens) — assess additive estrogenic load; (3) Other non-traditional therapies with potential endocrine interaction — document clearly.
Escalation / Referral Thresholds
  • Abnormal or unexplained uterine bleeding → Gynecology referral
  • Persistent spotting despite appropriate progesterone support → Gynecology
  • Known or suspected estrogen-sensitive malignancy → Pause therapy, oncology
  • New breast findings or concerning imaging → Pause therapy, further evaluation
Documentation Requirements
  • Indication and clinical rationale for estradiol therapy
  • Route selection and oversight considerations
  • Uterine status and endometrial protection strategy
  • Summary of shared decision-making
  • Ongoing reassessment of benefit and risk

Progesterone Therapy & Endometrial Protection
2.2 · Adult Female Patients · With or Without Concurrent Estrogen
Core Principles
  • Progesterone decline often precedes estrogen decline, particularly in perimenopause
  • Therapy is symptom- and pattern-driven, not lab-limited
  • Supports neurologic, metabolic, and reproductive balance
  • Goal is to restore physiologic signaling — not suppress normal function
Indications — With Estrogen Therapy
Required
Progesterone is required for ALL women with an intact uterus receiving systemic estradiol — to protect against endometrial hyperplasia, reduce estrogen-dominant effects, and support balanced hormone signaling.
Indications — Independent of Estrogen
Clinical ContextPresenting Patterns
Luteal Phase DeficiencyShortened cycles, premenstrual spotting, second-half symptom worsening
Sleep & Circadian DysregulationDifficulty falling/staying asleep, early morning waking, non-restorative sleep

Post-HysterectomySleep disturbance, anxiety, neurocognitive symptoms (when ovaries present or symptoms suggest benefit)
⚑ Provider Note — Pregnancy Test
A pregnancy test to rule out pregnancy (R/O pregnancy) is required before initiating progesterone therapy in any patient with an intact uterus and potential fertility.
Escalation / Referral
  • Abnormal uterine bleeding → Gynecology referral; pause therapy pending evaluation
  • Persistent or unexplained spotting → Gynecology
  • Suspected endometrial pathology → Pause therapy; gynecology workup

Testosterone Therapy for Men
2.3 · Injectable & Pellet · Adult Male Patients
Eligibility
  • Symptoms consistent with testosterone deficiency (fatigue, reduced libido, ED, mood changes, cognitive concerns, decreased muscle mass, impaired recovery)
  • Laboratory evidence of low or suboptimal testosterone levels relative to age and clinical context
  • Absence of absolute contraindications
  • Full evaluation of lifestyle factors completed prior to initiation
  • Willingness to comply with longitudinal monitoring requirements
Exclusions / Contraindications
  • Known or suspected prostate or breast cancer
  • Elevated PSA without appropriate evaluation
  • Hematocrit above acceptable thresholds
  • Untreated severe obstructive sleep apnea
  • Unstable cardiovascular disease or active thromboembolic disease
  • Desire for fertility without prior counseling
  • Strong family history of hormone-sensitive cancers — document and discuss risk before initiating
Treatment Modalities
Injectable Testosterone
IM or subcutaneous via FDA-approved or compounded formulations. Conservative initial dosing. Titration based on symptom response and lab monitoring. In-office or home administration with training.
Testosterone Pellet
Subcutaneous implantation in-office under sterile technique. For patients seeking longer-acting therapy. Sustained delivery over several months. Re-implantation timing based on symptom recurrence and labs.
TRT Monitoring Parameters
  • Symptom assessment and quality-of-life review
  • Serum total testosterone levels
  • Hematocrit and hemoglobin (see Polycythemia Pathway §2.5)
  • Lipid profile
  • PSA and prostate health monitoring per age and risk factors
  • Blood pressure and weight trends
Risk Mitigation Strategies
  • Dose or interval adjustment
  • Temporary suspension of therapy
  • Therapeutic phlebotomy or referral if indicated
  • Permanent discontinuation if safety thresholds exceeded
  • Referral to urology, cardiology, or other specialists when appropriate
Fertility Counseling — Required
All male patients must be counseled on the suppressive effects of testosterone therapy on spermatogenesis prior to initiation. Fertility-preserving alternatives and referral options must be discussed and documented.

Male Hypogonadism Evaluation Protocol
2.4 · Pathologic vs. Optimization-Based Differentiation
Definitions
Pathologic Hypogonadism
Medical diagnosis defined by compatible symptoms and consistently low testosterone due to testicular or hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction. Total testosterone <300 ng/dL on two morning measurements.
Testosterone Optimization
Symptom-guided, preventive approach for men with suboptimal levels and functional decline who do not meet disease-based thresholds. TT ~300-500 ng/dL with persistent symptoms, or low/borderline free T / adverse SHBG patterns.

Polycythemia Management Pathway
2.5 · TRT-Related · Thrombotic Risk Mitigation
Definition
Polycythemia is defined as a hematocrit exceeding established safety thresholds or a clinically significant rise from baseline attributable to testosterone therapy.
Risk Assessment Factors
  • Degree of hematocrit elevation
  • Presence of symptoms: headache, dizziness, flushing
  • Cardiovascular and thrombotic risk factors
  • Testosterone dose, formulation, and delivery modality
Management Strategies
  • Therapeutic phlebotomy when clinically indicated — preferred if staying on TRT
  • Dose reduction or extension of dosing interval
  • Temporary suspension of testosterone therapy
  • Transition to an alternative delivery modality
  • Referral to hematology or other specialists if required
Immediate Escalation Required If
Hematocrit continues to rise despite intervention · Thrombotic symptoms develop · Patient becomes non-adherent with monitoring

Peptide Therapy — Clinical Oversight Framework
3.1 · Governance · All Peptide Therapies
Clinical Oversight Principles
  • Peptides are signaling molecules that modulate physiologic pathways — not hormone replacements
  • Therapeutic intent is functional optimization, tissue support, or physiologic restoration — not disease substitution
  • Avoid polypharmacy or indiscriminate stacking
  • Risk-benefit considerations vary by peptide class, mechanism, and patient profile
  • Ongoing reassessment of efficacy, tolerability, and continued indication is essential
Peptide Categories Overview
CategoryPrimary Clinical FocusKey Oversight
Growth Hormone SecretagoguesBody composition, recovery, sleep, metabolic signalingMonitor IGF-1 trends, fluid retention, glucose response, long-term necessity
Metabolic PeptidesInsulin sensitivity, appetite signaling, fat metabolismCardiometabolic risk, GI tolerability, weight trajectory
Tissue Repair & RegenerativeInjury recovery, connective tissue, inflammation modulationTime-limited courses; reassess healing response
Neurocognitive / SleepSleep quality, stress response, cognitive supportMonitor neuropsychiatric response and sleep architecture

Valère Clinical Standards
  • Indication-driven, not trend-driven — select based on clinical appropriateness, not social media influence
  • Shared decision-making with documented counseling — mechanisms, outcomes, risks, and alternatives
  • Conservative starts, intentional titration — begin low, titrate methodically based on clinical endpoints
  • Clear stop-points and monitoring plans — define success, failure, and discontinuation criteria upfront
  • Avoid stacking without rationale — combination regimens require documented justification
Regulatory Note
Availability, compounding practices, and regulatory status vary by agent and jurisdiction. Providers must verify current regulatory standing and sourcing standards before prescribing.
🛡️
Safety Screening Requirements
3.2 · Required Before Any Peptide Initiation
Mandatory Screening Checklist
  • Pregnancy or Breastfeeding Status — Absolute contraindication for nearly all peptide therapies due to unknown fetal/infant effects and lack of safety data.
  • Active or Recent Malignancy — Many peptides have theoretical or demonstrated proliferative effects. Obtain oncologic history; consider oncology consultation for patients within 5 years of cancer diagnosis.
  • Thyroid Cancer or MEN2 History — Mandatory before GLP-1 receptor agonists (black box warning for medullary thyroid carcinoma). Include family history assessment.
  • Pituitary Pathology — Required before GH-axis peptides. Document history of pituitary adenoma, acromegaly, or sellar lesions. Consider baseline brain MRI if clinical suspicion exists.
  • Significant Cardiovascular Disease — Assess for uncontrolled hypertension, recent MI, unstable angina, heart failure, or arrhythmias. Particularly important for peptides affecting heart rate, BP, or metabolic demand.
  • Psychiatric Instability — Screen for active mood disorders, psychosis, or severe anxiety before prescribing central appetite suppressants or neuropeptides. Coordinate with mental health providers when appropriate.
Documentation Standard
All safety screening must be documented using standardized templates within the EHR. Contraindication overrides require attending physician approval and detailed clinical justification.

Immediate-Use IV Preparation Protocol
4.1 · Non-Compounding · Point-of-Care Preparation Only
This Protocol Applies To
  • FDA-approved, commercially manufactured IV base solutions (Normal Saline, Lactated Ringer's)
  • FDA-approved, commercially manufactured sterile injectable additives
  • Preparation performed at the point of care
  • Infusions initiated without delay after preparation
This Protocol Does NOT Apply To
  • Batch or advance preparation
  • Storage or refrigeration of prepared IV solutions
  • Assignment of beyond-use dates
  • Take-home injectable medications
  • Pharmacy-level sterile compounding or preparation for future use
Core Principles
  • Valère does not compound sterile products for storage or future administration
  • All IV additives introduced solely as immediate-use sterile preparations
  • Preparation occurs only when patient is present and ready for infusion
  • Administration begins immediately following preparation
  • Aseptic technique and patient safety take precedence over operational convenience
Therapy Principles
  • Each additive drawn using a new sterile syringe and needle
  • Vial stoppers and IV ports disinfected prior to access
  • Additives injected into IV base solution using aseptic technique
  • IV bags gently mixed per manufacturer guidance
  • Prepared solutions are not held, stored, or reused
  • Any unused or partially used solution discarded after infusion
⚑ Difficult IV Access Protocol
For difficult IV access: maximum 3 attempts per provider, maximum 1 provider making attempts before escalating. Document number of attempts and rationale in the medical record.
⚑ Patient Lifestyle Counseling — IV Therapy
Counsel all IV therapy patients on: (1) increase hydration in the days before and after infusion; (2) avoid alcohol prior to infusion (liver clearance); (3) limit high-fat meals around the time of IV administration to optimize absorption and minimize GI discomfort.
Documentation Requirements
  • IV base solution used
  • Sterile additives and dosages administered
  • Verification of expiration and integrity
  • Time of preparation and infusion initiation
  • Name and credentials of preparer/administrator
  • Patient tolerance and completion of therapy
Escalation Required If
Patient not present or ready for infusion · Supplies show compromised integrity or expiration · Intent to store or delay administration · Any deviation from immediate-use standards → escalate to clinical leadership immediately.

Most likely during: IV infusion · Injection · New supplement introduction
Recognize It
Hives, flushing, swelling (face/throat/tongue), difficulty breathing or stridor, hypotension, rapid or weak pulse, nausea/vomiting, loss of consciousness. Onset typically within minutes of exposure.
Response — Execute in This Order
  • Call 911 immediately. State: "We have a patient in anaphylactic shock at 313 Norton Drive, Suite 101B, Tallahassee FL 32308."
  • Stop the causative agent — discontinue IV infusion, remove any patch or device if applicable.
  • Epinephrine 0.3 mg IM (EpiPen) into the outer mid-thigh immediately. Repeat in 5–15 minutes if no improvement. Do not delay for any reason.
  • Position: Supine with legs elevated unless respiratory distress — then sit upright. Do not allow patient to stand.
  • Oxygen: Apply supplemental O₂ at 8–10 L/min via non-rebreather mask if available.
  • IV access: If already in place, open fluids wide. If not, attempt only if trained and time permits.
  • Diphenhydramine 25–50 mg IV or IM — after epinephrine, not instead of it.
  • Monitor continuously — BP, HR, RR, O₂ sat, mental status — until EMS arrives.
  • Meet EMS at the door. Give: time of exposure, agent involved, epinephrine doses and times, current vitals.
  • Document fully in patient chart. File incident report. Notify Melani immediately.
Post-Event
Patient must go to ER even if apparently recovered — biphasic anaphylaxis can occur 4–12 hours later. Do not discharge from office. Restock epinephrine immediately after use. Debrief team within 24 hours. Document causative agent and flag permanently in patient's allergy list.

Vasovagal Syncope / Fainting
Most likely during: Pellet procedure · Injection · Blood draw · Prolonged standing
Recognize Pre-Syncope
Pallor, diaphoresis, nausea, lightheadedness, tunnel vision, bradycardia. Act before the patient loses consciousness — pre-syncope is the intervention window.
Pre-Syncope Response
  • Stop the procedure immediately.
  • Lower patient to supine position immediately — recline table or assist to floor safely. Elevate legs above heart level.
  • Loosen constrictive clothing. Ensure airway is clear.
  • Cool cloth to forehead and back of neck. Call patient's name.
  • Have patient perform leg crossing or hand-gripping if still conscious.
  • Monitor BP and HR. Most episodes resolve within 1–2 minutes with positional change.
If Patient Loses Consciousness
  • Confirm unresponsiveness — call name, tap shoulder.
  • Call 911 if unresponsive more than 60 seconds or vitals are abnormal.
  • Check pulse and breathing — if absent, begin CPR and deploy AED immediately.
  • Check blood glucose — rule out hypoglycemia especially in GLP-1 patients.
  • Do not allow patient to stand until fully alert, normotensive, and asymptomatic for at least 10 minutes.
  • Document event including vitals, duration of LOC, and recovery timeline.
When to Send to ER
Syncope with injury from fall · Prolonged LOC (>2 minutes) · Associated chest pain or palpitations · No clear vasovagal trigger · First episode in patient over 60 · Patient on anticoagulants with any head trauma. When in doubt, send.

Severe GLP-1 / GI Reaction
Pancreatitis · Severe dehydration · Ileus · Bowel obstruction signs
Severity Tiers
Mild — Manage In Practice
Nausea, mild vomiting, reduced appetite in first 1–2 weeks. Expected. Counsel, reduce dose, slow titration.
Moderate — Urgent Evaluation
Persistent vomiting >24 hours, inability to keep fluids down, significant dehydration, abdominal pain beyond typical nausea.
Severe — 911 / ER Now
Severe epigastric pain radiating to back (pancreatitis), high fever, unable to stand, hypotension, altered mental status, rigid abdomen.
Response by Severity
  • Mild: Reduce next dose. Counsel on eating slowly, small meals, hydration. Do not escalate dose until symptoms resolved.
  • Moderate: Hold GLP-1. IV fluids in office if available and patient stable. If unable to manage — urgent care or ER for IV hydration and antiemetics. Recheck within 48 hours before resuming at lower dose.
  • Severe / Pancreatitis pattern: Call 911. Do not give food or water. IV access if available. Give EMS full medication history. Discontinue GLP-1 permanently if pancreatitis confirmed.
Pancreatitis — Never Resume
Confirmed pancreatitis = permanent discontinuation of GLP-1. Document as permanent contraindication in chart regardless of severity.

Cardiovascular Events
Chest pain · Palpitations · Hypertensive crisis · Stroke signs
Chest Pain — ACS Protocol
  • Call 911 immediately for any chest pain. State "possible cardiac event."
  • Have patient stop all activity — seat or lay down.
  • Aspirin 324 mg chewable — if not allergic and no contraindication. Have patient chew, not swallow whole.
  • Apply supplemental O₂ if O₂ sat below 94%.
  • Obtain vitals — BP both arms if possible, HR, O₂ sat.
  • Do not give food, water, or other medications without physician direction.
  • Stay with patient. Retrieve AED — ready at bedside. Do not apply unless pulseless.
  • Meet EMS with: patient name, age, medications, vitals, aspirin given Y/N and time.
Hypertensive Crisis (BP ≥ 180/120)
  • Recheck BP in 5 minutes in opposite arm after patient resting quietly.
  • With symptoms (headache, visual changes, chest pain, altered mental status) → Call 911 — hypertensive emergency.
  • Without symptoms → hypertensive urgency. Hold all therapies. Transport to ER within 1–2 hours. Do not attempt to rapidly lower BP in office.
Stroke — FAST + Call 911
F — Face
Ask patient to smile. Is one side drooping?
A — Arms
Ask patient to raise both arms. Does one drift down?
S — Speech
Ask patient to repeat a simple sentence. Slurred or strange?
T — Time = Call 911 NOW
Any positive FAST finding = call 911 immediately. Note exact time symptoms began. Do not give aspirin if stroke suspected — hemorrhagic stroke can be worsened by aspirin.

Serotonin Syndrome
Most likely: Tesofensine + SSRI · 5-HTP + SSRI · Multiple serotonergic agents
Recognize the Triad
1. Neuromuscular: tremor, clonus, hyperreflexia, myoclonus, ataxia, seizures. 2. Autonomic instability: hyperthermia, diaphoresis, tachycardia, hypertension, diarrhea. 3. Altered mental status: agitation, confusion, restlessness. Mild cases: tremor + tachycardia + diaphoresis — act immediately.
Response — Medical Emergency
  • Call 911 immediately. Serotonin syndrome can progress rapidly to hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, and death.
  • Discontinue ALL serotonergic agents — stop any infusion, remove any patch.
  • Position safely — protect from injury during agitation or myoclonus. Do not forcefully restrain.
  • Cool the patient if hyperthermia present — cool cloths, fan, remove clothing. No ice baths.
  • Monitor vitals continuously — temperature is the most critical parameter.
  • Do NOT give antipyretics — hyperthermia is from muscle activity, not infection. Will not help.
  • Give EMS: full medication list including all Rx, OTC, supplements, and substances.
After the Event
Full medication reconciliation required before any new prescription. Permanently document the precipitating combination in allergy/interaction section. Review all current patients on serotonergic medications for similar risks.

Psychiatric Emergency
Acute suicidality · Psychosis · Severe anxiety crisis · Aggressive behavior
Acute Suicidality or Self-Harm Disclosure
  • Stay calm and stay present. Do not leave the patient alone. Call for another staff member to assist without alarming the patient.
  • Listen without judgment. Acknowledge: "I hear that you're in a really difficult place and I'm glad you're telling me."
  • Assess immediate safety directly: "Are you thinking about hurting yourself right now?"
  • If immediate risk (plan, means, intent, actively threatening): Call 911 for Baker Act evaluation. Do not leave patient alone.
  • If significant but not immediate risk: Do not discharge without a safety plan. Contact Florida Crisis Line: (850) 523-3333. Involve support person with patient consent. Schedule urgent psychiatric follow-up before patient leaves.
  • Document fully: patient's words, your assessment, actions taken, resources provided, follow-up plan.
Severe Anxiety / Panic Attack
  • Move to quiet space. Dim lights. Reduce stimulation.
  • Grounding: name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear.
  • Slow breathing: in for 4 counts, hold 4, out for 6. Model it with them.
  • No medications without physician direction. Do not leave patient alone until fully recovered.
  • Post-resolution: assess whether a new Rx (Tesofensine, stimulating peptide) may have contributed. Adjust protocol as needed.

DEA Registration & Telehealth Prescribing
Schedule III-V · Testosterone · Telehealth flexibilities 2026
DEA Telehealth — 2026 Status
DEA telemedicine flexibilities for Schedule III-V controlled substance prescribing (including testosterone — Schedule III) extended through December 31, 2026. Practitioners with valid DEA registration may prescribe Schedule III-V via telemedicine without prior in-person examination provided: (1) legitimate medical purpose documented, (2) prescriber licensed in state where patient is physically located, (3) two-way audio-visual communication used (not audio-only for Schedule III-V). Verify current DEA rules before each prescribing encounter.
Key DEA Requirements
  • DEA registration is location-specific — tied to your primary registered address
  • Multi-state telehealth: must hold valid DEA registration in state where patient is physically located, OR operate under current telehealth flexibility rules
  • DEA renewal: every 3 years — set 90-day reminder. Lapsed registration = immediate halt to controlled substance prescribing
  • Schedule III (testosterone): 6-month supply maximum per Rx; up to 5 refills in 6 months
  • Florida PDMP (E-FORCSE) check required before every controlled substance prescription — document in chart
  • EPCS (electronic prescribing for controlled substances) required in Florida — verify EHR compliance
Telehealth Documentation — Controlled Substances
  • Document patient's state and city of location at time of each visit
  • Document that visit was conducted via two-way audio-visual communication
  • Document clinical rationale — identical standard to in-person visit
  • Pharmacy must be in the state where the patient is located

Florida APRN Prescribing Requirements
Collaborative practice · Scope · Formulary · PDMP · Telehealth
Key Requirements
  • Collaborative Practice Agreement (CPA): Florida APRNs must have a written CPA with a supervising physician specifying scope, prescribing authority, and consultation protocols. Keep current signed CPA on file and accessible.
  • APRN formulary: May prescribe within Florida Board of Nursing formulary. Updated periodically — verify for any new medication category.
  • Schedule II: Requires physician co-signature in Florida. Testosterone is Schedule III — no co-signature required.
  • EPCS: Electronic prescribing for controlled substances required in Florida. Verify EHR compliance.
  • PDMP (E-FORCSE): Check before every controlled substance Rx. Document check in chart. Legally required for Schedule II-IV in Florida.
  • Telehealth: Patient must be physically in Florida at visit time for Florida license to apply. Obtain and document telehealth informed consent at initiation of telehealth care.

Compounding Pharmacy Standards
503A vs. 503B · Quality verification · USP standards
503A — Traditional Compounding
Patient-specific prescriptions only. State pharmacy board regulated. USP <797> (sterile) or <795> (non-sterile). Subject to state inspection. Most hormone and peptide compounding pharmacies. Each Rx must be patient-specific.
503B — Outsourcing Facility
FDA regulated. Can produce larger batches without patient-specific Rx. Subject to FDA inspection and cGMP. Higher quality assurance standards. May handle some Category 2 compounds differently than 503A.
Pharmacy Vetting Checklist
  • Verify state pharmacy board license is active — check via state board website, not pharmacy's representation
  • Request Certificate of Analysis (COA) — potency, sterility, endotoxin testing from independent third-party lab
  • Confirm USP <797> compliance for all sterile preparations
  • PCAB accreditation (voluntary but indicates quality commitment)
  • Ask specifically about their Category 2 FDA peptide compliance position
  • Verify cold chain shipping — temperature logging for heat-sensitive compounds

HIPAA Compliance — Telehealth & Clinical Practice
PHI handling · Platforms · Communications · Breach protocol
Minimum Requirements
  • Notice of Privacy Practices: Provide to every new patient. Obtain signed acknowledgment. Keep on file.
  • Business Associate Agreements: Required with every vendor accessing PHI — EHR (Praxis), telehealth platform, lab companies, billing service, Fullscript, answering service. No BAA = no PHI access.
  • Access Controls: Unique login per staff member. No shared passwords. Access levels match role.
  • Device Security: Password protection, screen timeout, encryption on all PHI-access devices. No PHI on personal devices without MDM enrollment. Lost device = potential breach — report immediately.
  • Telehealth platform: Must have signed BAA, end-to-end encryption, HIPAA Security Rule compliance. Approved: Doxy.me, Zoom for Healthcare, Spruce. NOT approved: standard Zoom, FaceTime, Skype, Google Meet personal versions.
  • Text/email PHI: Must use HIPAA-compliant secure messaging (Spruce, OhMD, Klara). Standard SMS not HIPAA-compliant for PHI. Portal messages are the preferred method for all clinical communication.
Breach Response
  • Identify and contain the breach immediately.
  • Assess — what PHI, how many patients, likelihood of compromise.
  • Notify Melani immediately regardless of time of day.
  • Document everything — what happened, when, who discovered it, what was done.
  • Affected patients notified within 60 days. HHS notified within 60 days for 500+ record breaches, annually for smaller.
  • Consult healthcare attorney before any public notification.
